\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\note{D1 -- What the ladder changes: a network-prior claim should report \real{} vs \rewired{}
(and \textsc{labels}), not vs uniform random; cost is one extra training run per seed; toolkit released.}

\note{D2 -- Why degree is what models pick up: hub targets elicit larger responses; propagation on any
graph with the same degrees reproduces that mass (\cref{sec:theory}); publication bias explains part
of degree but not the response association (HuRI contrast).}

\note{D3 -- Where network structure is worth modelling: directed, signed regulatory networks carry
gene-level locality beyond degree (+0.03 to +0.07); target identity, morphology and a single measured
response carry far more drug-side information than undirected wiring. Direction for model builders:
signed regulatory structure on the gene side, target identity on the drug side.}

\note{D4 -- Scope (one short paragraph, stated as scope, not as weakness): chemical perturbations on
L1000/Tahoe plus GEARS on genetic perturbations; ladder applies unchanged to other graphs.}

\section{Conclusion}
\note{Three sentences: degree, not wiring, is what current networks contribute; the ladder makes the
distinction cheap; directed regulation and target identity are where the information is.}
